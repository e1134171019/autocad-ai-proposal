# Proposal Copywriting

## Overview

A proposal headline is the output of positioning, not the starting point. Build the buyer/offer model first, then write and compare copy.

## Required Inputs

Establish these before selecting copy:

1. **Buyer / ICP** — who decides, who uses, who reviews technically.
2. **Status quo / alternative** — what they do if they do not buy.
3. **Customer problem** — in the buyer's language, not internal jargon.
4. **Primary value** — what changes for the buyer.
5. **Differentiator** — why this approach instead of the alternative.
6. **Proof status** — proven, proposed, simulated, or unknown.
7. **Primary CTA** — one commercial next action.

If any item is unknown, mark it unknown; do not invent it.

## Message Ladder

Produce, in order:

- **Category:** what this is.
- **Value proposition:** what the buyer gets.
- **Three supporting pillars:** problem/value themes only.
- **Proof map:** evidence available for each pillar.
- **Claim boundary:** what must stay proposal-stage or concept-stage.

## Headline Matrix

Generate at least 5 headline candidates using different frames:

- category clarity;
- customer pain;
- desired outcome;
- differentiated approach;
- strategic/technology frame.

For every candidate include:

- headline;
- one-sentence mechanism subheadline;
- one primary CTA direction;
- claim-risk note.

Score each candidate 1–5 on **clarity, relevance, value, differentiation, credibility, conversion fit**. Recommend a winner only after comparison.

## Proposal Page Argument

Use the shortest sequence that completes the sales argument. For a complex B2B technical proposal, default to:

`Hero → Current Problem → Key Insight → Proposed Solution → Concept/Demo → What You Get → Why This Approach → Trust/Proof → Next Step`

Do not add sections merely because a framework contains them.

## Technical / AI Proposals

When AI is part of the offer:

- AI may be a first-class sales concept when it creates real buyer value.
- Attribute exact calculations, rules, geometry, safety logic, or deterministic outputs to the actual system responsible.
- Distinguish `implemented`, `planned`, `concept simulation`, and `proposed capability`.
- Do not fabricate customer proof, savings, accuracy, timelines, testimonials, guarantees, or production-readiness.

## Writing Rules

- Prefer buyer language over vendor language.
- Prefer concrete outcomes over feature labels.
- Prefer specific, defensible wording over adjectives such as “smart”, “revolutionary”, or “powerful”.
- One idea per section.
- Every feature must answer: **so what does the buyer gain?**
- Every major claim must answer: **why should the buyer believe it?**
- Keep one primary CTA for the page.

## Output Contract

Return:

1. positioning brief;
2. message ladder;
3. 5+ headline/subheadline/CTA variants with scores;
4. recommended hero with rationale;
5. page narrative outline;
6. claim-risk list;
7. unresolved evidence gaps.
